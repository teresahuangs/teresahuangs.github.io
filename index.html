About
Engineer first, product manager by choice.

I like problems where the hard part is translating what a person means into something a system can run. At DataVisor that meant watching fraud strategists draft detection logic in an external LLM and paste it back into our platform, and deciding the right product was an agent, not a redesign.

I write my own SQL, read the code I am asking for, and build the metrics that decide whether a feature worked. In 2024 I co-founded LearnSuite, a scheduling and payments product for tutoring centers that reached paying subscribers. Before that I spent four years as a software engineer and ML researcher across networking, geospatial imagery, and astronomy.

Selected work
What I owned, what I found, and what changed.
  1. DataVisor · 2025 to presentProduct owner

    AI Agents for rules and features

    DataVisor is a fraud and anti-money-laundering platform. Client transactions flow through a rules and features engine, and flagged activity lands with analysts as alerts. Writing those rules was technical enough that strategy work got deferred to support teams or the client’s engineers. My product metrics dashboards showed most support tickets pointed at that one surface, and exit interviews from churned clients named usability and onboarding time directly.

    We tried the obvious fixes first: UX firms, rebuilt documentation, more support hires, professional services. Direct observation showed the real bottleneck was translating intent into executable logic, and users were already doing that in outside LLMs. I owned the agent product from diagnosis through launch, letting clients express a strategy in natural language while keeping the platform’s full flexibility.
